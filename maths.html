Maths question bank (Edexcel 1MA1 Higher)

Edexcel 1MA1 Higher: frequent questions by topic with likelihood, full-marks methods, videos, topic papers and 23 worked answers.

The topics below appear on almost every set of three Higher papers; mastering the "Very high" rows secures roughly two-thirds of the marks, and the grade 8–9 rows decide the 9.

How to read the likelihood column. The 9–1 specification was first examined in 2017, so there are about nine summer series plus November resits, not ten years. Likelihood is an estimate of how often a topic appears somewhere across the three papers in a series, based on the pattern of past papers; it is not an official statistic. Very high = most series; High = about two series in three; Medium = about half; Lower = occasional but still examinable.

For every topic: watch the Corbettmaths video, do the practice questions, then do the Physics & Maths Tutor (PMT) topic paper and mark it with its answer sheet (swap QP for MA in the link). Full papers by year: PMT Paper 1, Paper 2, Paper 3, Maths Genie Edexcel papers and predicted papers.

Worked questions with full-mark answers

These are original questions written in the style that recurs on Edexcel Higher papers ("Recurring") or that tests examinable content in a newer way ("Potential"). The official past questions and mark schemes are on the PMT and Pearson links above; these show the working an examiner wants to see. Cover the answer, attempt, then compare line by line.

1. Number — Recurring (3 marks). Write 360 as a product of prime factors, then find the HCF and LCM of 360 and 84.

  • 360 = 2³ × 3² × 5; 84 = 2² × 3 × 7
  • HCF = 2² × 3 = 12; LCM = 2³ × 3² × 5 × 7 = 2520

2. Indices — Recurring, non-calculator (2 marks). Evaluate 27^(−2/3).

  • Cube root of 27 = 3; squared = 9; negative power = reciprocal → 1/9

3. Surds — Recurring (3 marks). Rationalise and simplify 6 / (3 − √3).

  • Multiply by (3 + √3)/(3 + √3): 6(3 + √3) / (9 − 3) = (18 + 6√3)/6 = 3 + √3

4. Recurring decimals — Recurring (3 marks). Prove that 0.27̇ recurring (0.272727…) = 3/11.

  • Let x = 0.2727…; 100x = 27.2727…
  • 100x − x = 99x = 27, so x = 27/99 = 3/11 (write the final line as a statement)

5. Bounds — Recurring (3 marks). Distance 120 m to the nearest 10 m; time 15.0 s to 1 decimal place. Find the upper bound of the speed.

  • UB distance 125 m; LB time 14.95 s
  • Upper bound speed = 125 ÷ 14.95 = 8.36 m/s (8.3612…)

6. Rearranging — Recurring (4 marks). Make x the subject of y = (2x + 3)/(x − 1).

  • y(x − 1) = 2x + 3 → xy − y = 2x + 3 → xy − 2x = y + 3 → x(y − 2) = y + 3
  • x = (y + 3)/(y − 2)

7. Quadratic formula — Recurring (3 marks). Solve 3x² − 7x − 2 = 0 to 2 decimal places.

  • a = 3, b = −7, c = −2: x = (7 ± √(49 + 24)) / 6 = (7 ± √73)/6
  • x = 2.59 or x = −0.26

8. Simultaneous with a quadratic — Recurring (5 marks). Solve y = 2x + 1 and x² + y² = 10.

  • x² + (2x + 1)² = 10 → 5x² + 4x − 9 = 0 → (5x + 9)(x − 1) = 0
  • x = 1, y = 3 and x = −9/5, y = −13/5 (both pairs needed)

9. Completing the square — Recurring (3 marks). Write x² − 6x + 11 in the form (x + a)² + b and state the turning point.

  • (x − 3)² − 9 + 11 = (x − 3)² + 2; turning point (3, 2)

10. Proof — Recurring (3 marks). Prove that the difference between the squares of two consecutive odd numbers is a multiple of 8.

  • Odd numbers 2n + 1 and 2n + 3
  • (2n + 3)² − (2n + 1)² = (4n² + 12n + 9) − (4n² + 4n + 1) = 8n + 8 = 8(n + 1), which is a multiple of 8

11. Functions — Recurring (3 marks). f(x) = 3x − 2, g(x) = x². Find fg(x) and f⁻¹(x).

  • fg(x) = f(x²) = 3x² − 2
  • y = 3x − 2 → x = (y + 2)/3 → f⁻¹(x) = (x + 2)/3

12. Quadratic sequence — Potential (3 marks). Find the nth term of 4, 11, 22, 37, …

  • First differences 7, 11, 15; second difference 4 → 2n²
  • Sequence − 2n²: 2, 3, 4, 5 → n + 1 → 2n² + n + 1

13. Quadratic inequality — Potential (3 marks). Solve x² − x − 12 < 0.

  • (x − 4)(x + 3) < 0; critical values −3 and 4; the parabola is below the axis between them → −3 < x < 4

14. Tangent to a circle — Potential (3 marks). Find the equation of the tangent to x² + y² = 25 at (3, 4).

  • Gradient of radius = 4/3, so tangent gradient = −3/4
  • y − 4 = −¾(x − 3) → y = −¾x + 25/4 (or 4y + 3x = 25)

15. Iteration — Recurring (4 marks). Show x³ + 4x − 1 = 0 has a root between 0 and 1, then use xₙ₊₁ = (1 − xₙ³)/4 with x₀ = 0.25 to find x₃.

  • At x = 0: −1; at x = 1: 4. Change of sign, so a root lies between 0 and 1
  • x₁ = 0.24609…, x₂ = 0.24627…, x₃ = 0.24627 (0.2463 to 4 d.p.)

16. Compound interest — Recurring (2 marks). £5000 at 3% compound interest for 4 years.

  • 5000 × 1.03⁴ = £5627.54

17. Reverse percentage — Recurring (2 marks). After a 20% reduction a coat costs £64. Find the original price.

  • 64 ÷ 0.8 = £80

18. Inverse proportion — Recurring (4 marks). y is inversely proportional to x²; y = 5 when x = 2. Find y when x = 4.

  • y = k/x² → 5 = k/4 → k = 20 → y = 20/x²; when x = 4, y = 1.25

19. Cosine rule and area — Recurring (5 marks). Triangle with a = 7 cm, b = 9 cm, angle C = 50°. Find side c and the area.

  • c² = 49 + 81 − 2(7)(9)cos 50° = 130 − 80.99 = 49.01 → c = 7.00 cm (3 s.f.)
  • Area = ½ × 7 × 9 × sin 50° = 24.1 cm²

20. Similar solids — Recurring (3 marks). Two similar solids have surface areas 50 cm² and 200 cm². The smaller has volume 30 cm³. Find the larger volume.

  • Area scale 4 → length scale 2 → volume scale 8 → 30 × 8 = 240 cm³

21. Vectors — Recurring (3 marks). OA = a, OB = b, M is the midpoint of AB. Find OM.

  • AB = b − a; OM = OA + ½AB = a + ½(b − a) = ½(a + b)

22. Tree diagram without replacement — Recurring (4 marks). A bag has 5 red and 3 blue counters. Two are taken without replacement. Find P(both the same colour).

  • P(RR) = 5/8 × 4/7 = 20/56; P(BB) = 3/8 × 2/7 = 6/56
  • Total = 26/56 = 13/28

23. Histograms — Recurring (2 marks). Class 10 < t ≤ 20 has frequency 30; class 20 < t ≤ 40 has frequency 30. Find each frequency density.

  • 30 ÷ 10 = 3; 30 ÷ 20 = 1.5 (bars of different heights, equal areas)
