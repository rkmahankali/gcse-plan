The goal and the strategy

The target is a grade 9 in every subject, which is achievable; 100% in every paper is not a realistic target and chasing it causes burnout. A grade 9 typically needs roughly 70–85% of the total marks (it varies by subject and year), so Aikya should aim to score 85–90%+ on timed past papers by April to give herself a safe buffer.

There are 28 weeks from today (26 Oct 2026) to the first exam on Monday 10 May 2027. The plan uses that time in four phases: fill gaps, master content, practise papers, peak for the exams.

Three corrections to the starting brief, found while checking the specifications:

  • Maths has 3 papers, not 2 (Edexcel 1MA1: Paper 1 non-calculator, Papers 2 and 3 calculator).
  • 3D Design has no written papers. Bulmershe teaches AQA GCSE 3D Design: a Personal Portfolio (60%) plus an Externally Set Assignment ending in a supervised practical exam (40%). It is won in the studio, not the exam hall.
  • PE has 2 written papers plus a practical. AQA 8582 = Paper 1 (30%) + Paper 2 (30%) + practical performance in three sports (40%).

The five rules that produce 9s:

  1. Test, don't re-read. Every session starts with recall (flashcards, blurting, questions) before looking at notes.
  2. Space it. Each topic is revisited after 1 day, 1 week, 1 month.
  3. Past papers are the main course from February, always marked strictly against the official mark scheme.
  4. Fix mistakes, not strengths. Every lost mark goes into the Mistakes Log and is re-tested.
  5. Mark-scheme language. Learn how examiners award marks for each question type (section 'How to answer for full marks').

Weekly routine and the 50-minute session

Every subject is touched at least twice a week, with Maths, the sciences and English getting the most time because they carry the most papers. This is the Phase 1–3 week; from February, swap one weekday block per subject for a timed paper.

Day Block 1 (50 min) Block 2 (50 min) Daily extra (15 min)
Monday Maths Biology Flashcards (Anki/Seneca)
Tuesday English Literature (quotes + one paragraph) Chemistry Flashcards
Wednesday History Physics Flashcards
Thursday Maths English Language (one reading or writing question) Flashcards
Friday PE theory 3D Design sketchbook/portfolio Light: plan weekend
Saturday Timed past paper (rotate Maths / a science) History or English essay under time Mark both against mark schemes
Sunday 3D Design (practical work, annotations) Mistakes Log re-test + weekly tracker update (30 min) Rest afternoon

How every 50-minute block runs:

  1. Blurt (5 min): close the book and write everything she remembers about the topic.
  2. Check and fill (5 min): compare with the knowledge organiser; add missing points in a different colour.
  3. Exam questions (25 min): past-paper questions on that topic only (Physics & Maths Tutor or Save My Exams topic packs).
  4. Mark and log (10 min): strict marking with the official mark scheme; every lost mark goes into the Mistakes Log.
  5. Flashcards (5 min): turn the new mistakes into cards.

Phones live in another room during blocks. A 10-minute break between blocks; no revision after 9:30 pm on school nights.

Maths — Edexcel 1MA1 (Higher tier, 3 papers)

Three 80-mark papers of 1h 30m each, equally weighted; any topic can appear on any paper. Grade 9 is won on the last 6–8 questions of each paper, so from January at least one session a week is grade 8–9 questions only.

Chapter Topics to master Grade 8–9 "killer" questions to drill
Number Fractions, indices, surds, standard form, bounds, recurring decimals, product of primes Rationalising surds; upper/lower bounds in calculations; recurring decimal to fraction proof
Algebra (largest share) Expanding/factorising, equations, simultaneous (incl. one quadratic), inequalities, sequences, graphs, functions Algebraic proof; iteration; composite and inverse functions; quadratic inequalities; graph transformations; tangent to a circle
Ratio, proportion and rates Ratio problems, % change, compound interest, direct/inverse proportion, speed/density/pressure Gradient of a curve and area under a graph as rates; proportion with powers
Geometry and measures Angles, polygons, Pythagoras, trig (incl. sine/cosine rule, 3D), circle theorems, transformations, vectors, volume/surface area, similarity, constructions Circle theorem proofs; vector proofs; 3D trigonometry; similar shapes area/volume
Probability Tree diagrams, Venn diagrams, set notation, conditional probability Conditional probability leading to a quadratic
Statistics Averages, cumulative frequency, box plots, histograms, sampling, scatter Histograms with unequal widths; comparing distributions

Paper-by-paper plan:

  • Paper 1, non-calculator (14 May): daily 10-minute arithmetic fluency (fractions, surds, long division, trig exact values). Do every Paper 1 since 2017 without a calculator.
  • Papers 2 and 3, calculator (27 May, 14 Jun): learn efficient calculator use (table function for iteration, fraction button, memory). Do every Paper 2 and 3 since 2017, plus November papers.

Grade 9 routine: Corbettmaths 5-a-day (Higher Plus) every weekday morning; Maths Genie grade 8/9 question packs on Saturdays; 2 full papers a week from February; target 220+/240 by Easter.

English Language — Edexcel 1EN0 (2 papers)

Half of all marks (80 of 160) are for Aikya's own writing, so a polished, rehearsed writing style is the fastest route to a 9. Reading questions reward precise analysis of specific words and structural choices.

Paper Question Marks Time What earns full marks
Paper 1: Fiction (24 May, 1h 45m, 40%) Q1 Identify 1 2 min Short, exact answer from the named lines
Q2 Explain 2 3 min Two quoted points, briefly explained
Q3 Language and structure 6 12 min 3 analytical points: quote, technique, effect on reader, zoom into single words
Q4 Evaluate a statement 15 25 min Agree/partly agree with clear judgement; evaluate how well the writer creates the effect; cover the whole section
Section B Imaginative writing 40 (16 SPaG) 55 min 5-minute plan; controlled narrative or description; varied sentence openings, ambitious vocabulary, deliberate structure (cyclical ending)
Paper 2: Non-fiction (8 Jun, 2h 05m, 60%) Q1, Q2, Q4, Q5 Retrieval 1–2 each 8 min total Quick and precise
Q3 Analyse text 1 15 18 min Language and structure, judgement on writer's purpose
Q6 Evaluate text 2 15 18 min How successfully the writer achieves the effect
Q7a Synthesise 6 7 min Similarities/differences in both texts, concise
Q7b Compare 14 18 min Compare ideas and methods, linking both texts in every paragraph
Section B Transactional writing 40 (16 SPaG) 45 min Correct form (article, letter, speech, report, review); audience and purpose clear; rhetorical devices; counter-argument

Preparation plan:

  • Oct–Dec: one writing piece a week alternating fiction/transactional; build a personal bank of 30 ambitious words and 10 sentence structures.
  • Jan–Mar: one full Section A under time every week; read one 19th-century short story and one quality opinion article each week.
  • Apr–Jun: full papers in time; perfect SPaG (semicolons, colons, dashes, paragraphing). Use the school's GCSE writing tasks booklet.

English Literature — Edexcel 1ET0 (2 papers, closed book)

Both papers are closed book, so memorised quotations are the foundation; grade 9 then comes from a clear argument about the writer's purpose, woven with context. Bulmershe's GCSE resources point to Macbeth, Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde and An Inspector Calls; confirm these and the poetry collection with Aikya's English teacher.

Paper Section Text Task Marks Time
Paper 1 (12 May, 1h 45m, 50%) A: Shakespeare Macbeth (a) extract analysis + (b) whole-play essay 20 + 20 55 min
B: Post-1914 drama An Inspector Calls One essay from a choice of two 32 + 8 SPaG 50 min
Paper 2 (19 May, 2h 15m, 50%) A: 19th-century novel Jekyll and Hyde (a) extract analysis + (b) whole-novel essay 20 + 20 55 min
B Part 1: Anthology poetry 15-poem collection (Relationships, Conflict, Time and Place or Belonging) Compare a named poem with one of her choice 20 40 min
B Part 2: Unseen poetry Two unseen poems Compare 20 40 min

Text-by-text checklist (tick each in the tracker):

  • Macbeth: act-by-act plot; 6 characters; themes (ambition, guilt, kingship, the supernatural, gender); Jacobean context (James I, Gunpowder Plot, divine right); 30 short quotes.
  • An Inspector Calls: each character's arc; themes (social responsibility, class, age, gender); 1912 vs 1945 context; Priestley's socialism; dramatic devices (lighting, entrances, the Inspector as mouthpiece); 25 quotes.
  • Jekyll and Hyde: chapter-by-chapter plot; duality, repression, reputation, science vs religion; Victorian context (Darwin, Gothic, Victorian respectability); 25 quotes.
  • Anthology: all 15 poems — each with poet, context, form, 3 key quotes, and 2 ideal comparison partners.
  • Unseen poetry: one unseen pair a week from January.

Grade 9 essay habits: a thesis in the first sentence; each paragraph opens with an idea about the writer's intention, not the plot; 3–4 embedded quotes per paragraph; zoom into single words; alternative interpretations ("perhaps", "alternatively"); context tied to the point, never bolted on.

History — Edexcel 1HI0 (3 papers, Bulmershe units)

Bulmershe teaches four units across three papers: Crime and Punishment with Whitechapel, the Cold War, Henry VIII, and Weimar and Nazi Germany (school History page). Henry VIII and the Cold War are taught this year, so Aikya should pre-read them now to get ahead. Note that the Cold War exam covers 1941–91, not just the 1958–70 crises. Edexcel changed some question formats from 2025 and removed some content from 2026; the History bank page lists both.

3D Design — AQA Art and Design (3D Design)

There are no written papers: a Personal Portfolio (60%) and an Externally Set Assignment that ends in a 10-hour supervised practical (40%), both submitted by 31 May 2027 (Bulmershe DT page). Both are marked against four equally weighted Assessment Objectives, so a 9 needs "exceptional" evidence in all four, not just a beautiful final piece.

Assessment Objective What the moderator looks for Aikya's evidence checklist
AO1 Develop Ideas informed by designers, architects and artists 3–4 designers per project analysed in her own words; her work clearly responds to them
AO2 Refine Experiments with materials, techniques and processes Material tests, maquettes, iterations, CAD (Affinity Photo, 2D Design) and laser-cut parts, each annotated with what worked and why
AO3 Record Observations and ideas through drawing, photography, notes Primary photographs, observational drawing, technical drawings, reflective annotations
AO4 Present A personal, meaningful final outcome that links to the journey A resolved final piece with clear links back to research and development

Timeline:

  • Now – Dec: ask Mrs Trenwith for the current mark of each portfolio project by AO; fix the weakest AO first; photograph everything.
  • Jan: AQA releases the ESA paper; choose the starting point that gives most scope for her strengths; mind-map, research two to three designers.
  • Feb – Apr: preparatory period — experiments, models, CAD, a rehearsed plan for the final piece; trial-run the hardest making steps.
  • ESA sessions (school sets dates): 10 supervised hours to make the final outcome exactly as rehearsed.
  • Weekly: two hours on Friday/Sunday plus Monday 3–4 pm DT intervention sessions at school.

How to answer each question type for full marks

Marks are awarded by mark schemes, so Aikya should answer each question type with a fixed formula that matches how examiners credit it. Practise each formula until it is automatic, then check every past-paper answer against the examiner's mark scheme and examiner report.

Subject Question type The full-marks formula
Maths Any multi-mark question Write every step: method marks are given even if the final answer is wrong; state units; round only at the end
Maths "Show that" / proof Every line justified; finish with a concluding sentence that matches the question
Sciences Calculation Equation → substitute with units → rearrange → answer with unit and sensible significant figures
Sciences 4–6 mark "explain" Linked chain of reasoning: cause → "which means" → effect; one point per mark plus one spare
Sciences "Evaluate" / "compare" Points for, points against (or both items side by side), then a justified conclusion
Sciences Required practical Method in numbered steps; independent, dependent, control variables; one risk; how to improve accuracy/repeatability
English Lang/Lit Analysis paragraph Idea about the writer's intention → short embedded quote → technique → zoom on one word → effect on reader → link to context/purpose
English Lang Writing tasks 5-minute plan; strong opening hook; 5–7 crafted paragraphs; range of punctuation; 5 minutes proofreading
History Describe one feature (2), asked twice One feature + one specific supporting detail (from 2025 this is two separate 2-mark questions)
History Explain why (12) Three factors (use both stimulus points + one of her own); each: point → precise evidence (dates, names, numbers) → explain how it caused the outcome
History How far do you agree (16) Two paragraphs for, one to two against, then a judgement that weighs the factors and says why one matters most
History Source usefulness (8) For each source: content, provenance (nature, origin, purpose), and own contextual knowledge to test it
History Interpretations (4 + 4 + 16) Quote both interpretations; explain differences by the sources/focus each historian used; final essay uses both plus own knowledge
PE 6–9 mark extended Knowledge (define), application (named sport example), evaluation (strengths, limits, conclusion)

In the exam room: spend a mark-to-time ratio (about 1 minute per mark); never leave a question blank; move on after 2 minutes stuck; leave 10 minutes to check units, signs, spelling and unanswered parts.

Wellbeing, the parent's role, and open questions

Tired students lose marks to careless errors, so rest is part of the plan, not a reward for finishing it. Protect 8–9 hours of sleep, one free evening a week, daily exercise, and no revision after 9:30 pm.

How parents can help most:

  • Join the Sunday 5-minute review; praise effort and fixed mistakes, not just scores.
  • Quiz flashcards for 10 minutes on car journeys or after dinner.
  • Keep the study space quiet and phone-free during blocks.
  • Book a teacher conversation early when a subject stays below 75% for two weeks.
  • Watch for stress signs (poor sleep, skipped meals, withdrawal) and ease the load if they appear; the school's mental health resources can help.
