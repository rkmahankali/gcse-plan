PE Paper 2 — Socio-cultural influences and well-being (20 May)

Topic Most frequent questions Likelihood Full-marks tip
Skill and ability Classify a skill on continua (open/closed, basic/complex, self-paced/externally paced, gross/fine) Very high Place it AND justify with the sporting context
Goal setting and information processing SMART targets; input → decision → output → feedback model High Apply each letter or stage to the named performer
Guidance and feedback Visual, verbal, manual, mechanical guidance; types of feedback for beginners vs elite Very high Recommend + justify for the performer's level
Arousal and mental preparation Inverted-U theory; stress management (deep breathing, visualisation, mental rehearsal) High Draw and label the curve when asked to explain
Commercialisation Golden triangle; sponsorship and media — advantages and disadvantages Very high Balanced answer for each stakeholder: performer, sport, sponsor, spectator
Ethics and deviance Sportsmanship vs gamesmanship; performance-enhancing drugs (types, effects, side effects); spectator behaviour Very high Name the drug class, the performer who would use it, and one side effect
Participation Factors affecting participation (gender, age, ethnicity, disability, socio-economic) High Give a factor and a specific strategy to increase participation
Health, fitness, well-being Physical, emotional, social health; sedentary lifestyle; obesity; somatotypes; diet, energy and hydration High Link each to its effect on performance

6- and 9-mark answers: knowledge (define), application (a specific sport and position), analysis/evaluation (advantages, limitations, conclusion). A 9-mark answer without a justified conclusion rarely reaches the top level.

3D Design — AO evidence checklist

The Personal Portfolio (60%) and the Externally Set Assignment (40%) are both marked against four equally weighted Assessment Objectives, and a moderator must be able to see each one on the page (Bulmershe DT page · AQA Art and Design).

AO What "exceptional" looks like Pages to have in every project
AO1 Develop ideas through investigation of sources Ideas clearly shaped by designers and architects she has analysed critically 2–3 designer studies with her own analysis (form, material, function, context), and a page showing how each influenced her ideas
AO2 Refine through experimenting with media and processes Purposeful, varied experiments that lead somewhere Material tests, maquettes, CAD and laser-cut trials, each annotated: what I tried, what worked, what I changed next
AO3 Record ideas and observations Skilled, varied recording Primary photography, observational and technical drawing, mood boards, reflective annotation throughout
AO4 Present a personal, meaningful response A resolved outcome that connects back to the whole journey Final piece photographed professionally from several angles, with a final evaluation linking to research

ESA plan (from January): read all AQA starting points; mind-map three; choose the one with the richest designer links; complete AO1–AO3 work in the preparation period; trial-run the final piece; then make it in the 10 supervised hours.

Designer ideas for an architectural/spatial focus: Zaha Hadid, Thomas Heatherwick, Frank Gehry, Tadao Ando, Charles and Ray Eames. Ask the teacher which fit her project best.

Worked PE questions with full-mark answers

Original questions in the AQA style; official papers and mark schemes are on the AQA link above.

PE1 Cardiac output (2). A performer's resting heart rate is 70 bpm and stroke volume 70 ml. Calculate cardiac output.

  • Q = SV × HR = 70 × 70 = 4900 ml/min = 4.9 l/min

PE2 Joints and movement (3). Identify the joint type and the movement at the knee during the upward phase of a squat.

  • Hinge joint
  • Extension
  • Agonist: quadriceps (hamstrings relax as antagonist)

PE3 Levers (3). Identify the lever class at the ankle when a sprinter is on their toes, and explain its mechanical advantage.

  • Second-class lever (load between fulcrum and effort: fulcrum at the ball of the foot, load = body weight, effort = gastrocnemius)
  • Effort arm is longer than load arm
  • High mechanical advantage: a large load can be moved with a small effort

PE4 Training (4). Explain why interval training suits a football midfielder.

  • Periods of high-intensity work followed by rest/recovery
  • Matches the intermittent demands of a match (sprints then jogging)
  • Develops both aerobic and anaerobic fitness
  • Improves recovery between sprints, so performance is maintained into the second half

PE5 Skill classification (3). Place a tennis serve on the open–closed continuum and justify.

  • Towards closed
  • The server controls the timing and the environment is stable
  • Little need to adapt to opponents at the moment of execution

PE6 Guidance (2). Suggest the best guidance for a beginner learning a forward roll.

  • Manual guidance (physical support from the coach)
  • Builds confidence and safety while the performer feels the correct movement

PE7 9-marker: Evaluate the use of sponsorship for an elite performer.

Knowledge: Sponsorship is financial or material support given in return for promotion of a company's brand.

Application and analysis (positive): For an elite tennis player, sponsorship funds coaching, travel and equipment, allowing full-time training; this should improve performance and ranking. It also raises the performer's profile, attracting further income.

Application and analysis (negative): Contracts may require media appearances that reduce training and rest time; the performer may have to promote products that conflict with their values (e.g., fast food); pressure to keep results high to retain sponsors can increase stress, and in extreme cases could tempt deviance.

Evaluation and conclusion: Overall, sponsorship benefits the elite performer more than it harms them, because the funding makes elite-level training possible; however, the performer must manage commitments carefully so that pressure and time demands do not damage performance.

Why it scores 9: clear definition (AO1), a named sport applied throughout (AO2), both sides analysed and a justified judgement (AO3).
